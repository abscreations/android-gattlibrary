V10 nearly became the first entry the register could not resolve, and the way it was actually
resolved is the useful part. It was opened on the belief that a proprietary frame with two
readers and no specification has no third source. That was false: **the captured corpus is a
third source.** Ten real frames constrain a field far more tightly than either project's prose
about it, and the constraint was already sitting in a file both projects ship.

The general lesson for later phases — several of which will hit exactly this shape — is to
interrogate the fixture corpus before declaring a field unsettleable. Ask what each candidate
reading predicts about bytes the other reading claims are unused, then go and look. A field is
only genuinely undecidable when the captured data is consistent with both readings, and that
is rarer than it first appears.

V4 is the first entry opened against a **specification** rather than a second project, which
§1.5.1 permits and D25 arguably requires: a published wire format is a stronger source than
either implementation, and an implementation that departs from it silently is exactly the kind
of inherited assumption a port carries forward by accident.

New entries are added as each phase's cross-source comparison table is built. An entry may be
opened by any of: differing identifiers, differing offsets or widths, differing scale factors
or units, differing state-machine ordering, or one project applying a correction the other does
not.

**An empty register for a multi-source family is itself suspicious.** Two independent
reverse-engineering efforts rarely agree on everything; complete agreement is weak evidence
that one derived from the other, which §1.5 says drops the family to tier 1.
